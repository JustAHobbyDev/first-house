# Winter paper-record storage recommendation

Disposable development report. Not House canon.

## Recommendation and reasoning

**Choose the North Closet for all 12 crates.** This is my inference from the
documents together, applying moisture protection, capacity, weekly access, and
then temperature stability in the requested order. No supplied document alone
directs this choice. The North Closet is not the driest option, nor the most
temperature-stable; it is the best supported compromise among locations that
can accommodate this shipment without removing the protected working desk. [S][E][O]

## Why the alternatives are worse — inference

The Cellar's capacity and superior temperature stability do not outweigh its
higher sustained humidity and history of water ingress under the stated priority
ordering. Drainage work and the absence of recent standing water do not establish
that future moisture problems are resolved. The staircase also makes weekly
consultation of approximately two crates less practical. [S][E][O]

The East Study has the strongest documented dryness, but only 10 available crate
spaces under the instruction to retain its desk. It cannot hold the complete
12-crate shipment on those terms. Splitting the shipment is less consistent with
the operations note when the North Closet can hold all twelve. Its greater
temperature variation is an additional disadvantage, not the primary reason to
exclude it. [S][E][O]

## Residual risk and limits

The North Closet's unexplained post-storm rise to **71% RH** is a real residual
risk. The log says the elevated reading lasted approximately six hours and
returned below 60% within eight hours, with no liquid water observed. It does not
establish the cause or rule out recurrence. Monitoring humidity, especially after
storms and as winter begins, is my recommendation, not an action already taken
or a documented remedy. These summer observations do not guarantee winter
conditions or establish an archival-safety threshold. [E][S]

Curled labels elsewhere in the cellar last winter are documented, but their cause
was not investigated and no environmental measurements were available. They are
an unresolved observation, not proof that humidity caused damage in this alcove. [O]

The survey describes afternoon sunlight through east-facing windows. I retain
that description without attempting to explain or correct it; the measured
19–27°C range supports the temperature comparison independently. [S][E]
